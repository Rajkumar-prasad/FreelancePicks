# Store Launch Kit email sequence

8 emails over 14 days for people who download the Store Launch Kit. Set them up in systeme.io as a campaign triggered by the signup tag (`SYSTEME_TAG_ID`).

Notes before you load them:
- Replace `{first_name}` with systeme.io's first name tag, and add a fallback like "there" for people who skipped the name field.
- Links go to FreelancePicks guides, not straight to Fiverr. The guides carry the affiliate buttons and give context, which converts better and keeps email links within Fiverr's affiliate terms. Check the terms in your affiliate dashboard if you ever want to link to Fiverr directly from email.
- Send from a real name ("Raj from FreelancePicks") and a domain email you have authenticated in systeme.io.
- Every email needs your unsubscribe link and postal address footer (systeme.io adds these).
- Keep the regular hyphen style. No em dashes.

## Email 2 - Day 1

**Subject:** The one thing to fix before you spend on ads
**Preview:** If this is wrong, every ad decision is a guess

Hi {first_name},

I have spent 7+ years running Meta and Google ads for ecommerce brands, and the most expensive mistake I see is simple: running ads before tracking works.

If your Pixel misses purchases or counts them twice, Meta optimizes toward the wrong people, and you cannot tell which ads make money.

The fix has two parts:

1. **Meta Pixel** in the browser
2. **Conversions API** from your server, with matching event IDs so each purchase is counted once

Here is the plain-English explanation: [Meta Pixel vs Conversions API](https://freelancepicks.com/guides/meta-pixel-vs-conversions-api/)

Then place one test order and check that the Purchase event shows up once in Events Manager, with the right value.

Not technical? This is one of the cheapest jobs to outsource, often $80 to $250. Here is what a proper setup includes and the red flags to avoid: [Pixel and CAPI setup guide](https://freelancepicks.com/hire/meta-pixel-capi-setup/)

Raj

## Email 3 - Day 3

**Subject:** Your product photos are doing most of the selling
**Preview:** You probably do not need a photographer yet

Hi {first_name},

Shoppers cannot touch your product, so your images do most of the selling, on the product page and in every ad.

Good news: you probably do not need a studio shoot yet. A modern phone, good daylight and professional editing get most new stores 90% of the way there.

Aim for 4 to 6 images per product:

- Main shot on a clean white background
- 2 to 3 other angles
- A close-up of materials or details
- An in-use or scale shot

Editing often costs just a few dollars per image. Order a 2-image test first, because quality varies a lot between sellers: [How to hire a product photo editor](https://freelancepicks.com/hire/product-photography/)

Raj

## Email 4 - Day 5

**Subject:** DIY or hire: setting up your store
**Preview:** What a Shopify setup really costs

Hi {first_name},

Should you build the store yourself or hire someone?

A simple rule: if you are comfortable with tech and selling a few products, a free theme set up by you is fine. If you have many products, variants, or just no time, hiring pays for itself.

Typical prices for a Shopify setup on freelance marketplaces:

- Basic: $100 to $300 (theme and a handful of products)
- Standard: $300 to $800 (full store: pages, policies, payments, shipping)
- Premium: $800 to $2,500+ (custom work and migrations)

The full breakdown, including plan fees and payment fees: [How much does it cost to set up a Shopify store?](https://freelancepicks.com/guides/shopify-store-setup-cost/)

One rule if you hire: use Shopify's collaborator access. Never share your password.

Raj

## Email 5 - Day 7

**Subject:** Does your logo survive at 32 pixels?
**Preview:** A quick test before you pay for a logo

Hi {first_name},

Your logo will appear in the corner of every ad, as your favicon and on your social profiles. Most of the time it is tiny.

Quick test: shrink it to 32 pixels wide and put it on a dark background. If it turns into a smudge, it needs a simpler design.

You do not need an agency at launch. A clean, original logo with vector files usually costs $80 to $200 on a freelance marketplace.

What each price level gets you: [How much does a logo cost?](https://freelancepicks.com/guides/logo-design-cost/)

## Email 6 - Day 9

**Subject:** The cheapest way to find a winning ad
**Preview:** Change the first 3 seconds, not the whole video

Hi {first_name},

Short videos are often the best-performing creatives on Meta and TikTok, especially UGC: a real person using your product on camera.

Here is the trick most new stores miss. Do not order 5 different videos. Order 2 or 3 videos, each with 3 different hooks (the first 3 seconds). Testing hooks is the cheapest way to find what stops the scroll.

What to budget:

- Most creators: $150 to $300 per video
- Paid ad usage rights: often an extra 30% to 50%

Make sure usage rights are included in writing.

Full pricing and a brief template: [How much does a UGC video cost?](https://freelancepicks.com/guides/ugc-video-cost/)

Raj

## Email 7 - Day 11

**Subject:** 6 rules I follow when hiring freelancers
**Preview:** Most hiring disasters are avoidable

Hi {first_name},

Most bad freelance experiences come from the same few mistakes. These rules prevent nearly all of them:

1. Keep messages and payments on the platform
2. Never share passwords. Use collaborator access (Shopify) and partner access (Meta).
3. Start with a small paid test or sample
4. Agree on deliverables, revisions and deadline in writing
5. Own your accounts: store, domain, ad account and pixel
6. Check recent reviews from projects like yours

Wondering where to hire? Here is a fair comparison of the two biggest platforms for store owners: [Fiverr vs Upwork](https://freelancepicks.com/guides/fiverr-vs-upwork-for-store-owners/)

Raj
